# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Prism serves people who need to understand Stellar and Soroban activity without losing access to the underlying evidence.

Primary users include curious users who want plain-language explanations, operators and builders who need protocol and contract context, investigators and analysts who look for behavior patterns and anomaly clues, and power users who need raw operations, events, diffs, storage, and exact transaction metadata.

Users often arrive with a hash, account, contract, asset, or question. Their job is to quickly understand what happened, who was involved, why Prism reached that interpretation, what stands out, and what to inspect next.

## Product Purpose

Prism is a Soroban-first block explorer for the Stellar network. It turns raw blockchain data into understandable, trustworthy stories while preserving auditability.

Success means a non-expert can understand the meaning of a transaction, contract, account, or ledger without needing protocol fluency, while an expert can still inspect the raw evidence and verify every conclusion.

## Brand Personality

Clear, trustworthy, investigative.

The product should feel calm, precise, and evidence-led. It should explain without condescending, surface uncertainty honestly, and help users move from summary to proof with confidence.

## Anti-references

Prism should not feel like a raw hash dump, crypto-neon trading screen, generic blockchain explorer clone, or over-decorated AI dashboard.

Avoid interfaces that hide evidence behind summaries, overuse futuristic effects, make everything look equally urgent, or treat blockchain data as decoration rather than something users need to reason about.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with meaning, then evidence. Start with what happened in human language, then make the supporting facts easy to inspect.
2. Preserve trust through visible reasoning. Classifications, confidence, heuristics, and uncertainty should be explicit rather than implied.
3. Serve both skim and audit modes. Casual users need a clear path through the story; experts need dense raw detail without fighting the interface.
4. Make actors and relationships primary. People understand roles, counterparties, contracts, and behavior better than operation taxonomies alone.
5. Progressive disclosure over simplification. Reduce initial cognitive load without removing advanced data or making users feel trapped in a simplified view.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target at least WCAG AA. Support reduced motion, keyboard navigation, visible focus states, sufficient contrast, and color-blind-safe status communication.

State must not rely on color alone. Dense technical views should remain scannable with strong labels, predictable structure, and plain-language summaries.
